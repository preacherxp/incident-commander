# Hands-on playtest — 19 September 2026

Played one complete four-incident run through the browser UI, choosing cards manually from the visible hand. Seed: 90210. First three incidents at 1440×900; Tuesday at 390×844. This is a design assessment from one informed playthrough, not evidence of general player enjoyment or mobile device performance.

**Assessment: 6/10 for engagement.** The rules are accessible and matching counters is satisfying. The Three.js arena gives the game a much stronger identity. Decisions become repetitive before the run ends, and the current balance rarely makes survival feel uncertain.

## What worked

- Three focus and a fresh hand keep turns short and understandable.
- Counter labels and the updated impact forecast make the immediate benefit obvious. Countering Friday's memory leak and Tuesday's infrastructure attack felt rewarding.
- The glowing arena, shield domes, attack beam, and orbit controls make the world appealing. Native card controls remain readable and usable alongside it.
- Tuesday remained playable at phone width, with the hand and End round control close together.

## Improvements, in priority order

1. **Make counterplay compete with rushing stability.** I usually chose the largest immediate stability gain and won comfortably. Tuesday was faster than the opening incident after drafting stronger cards. Give later incidents distinct, visible rules that create a reason to defend; for example, a clearly announced attack that also stalls recovery unless countered. Tune against both rush and defensive play, retaining a forgiving opening. Simply increasing harm everywhere would not create better choices.

2. **Make sudden attacks understandable at resolution.** Monday's fully countered opening threat was followed by Data corruption: −10 stability and +20 harm. The next round added an unannounced Vendor outage. The warning exists, but the prominent feedback shows numerical changes while the attack names require the log. Show the event name and effect together, and indicate surprise risk before ending the round. Keep the resolution short and skippable.

3. **Give the deck a direction earlier.** Immediate-value drafts were easy picks over slow investments such as War room when incidents last three or four rounds. Try a small choice of starting specialties, then tune rewards to support them. Rework the free runbook: drawing one card for zero focus was an automatic extra click. Paid draw was hard to justify against immediate progress. A card that makes the next specific action stronger would create a more interesting sequence.

4. **Reward strong play without encouraging cleanup clicks.** I healed immediately before the winning card in all four incidents to improve the result. S rank was generous despite Monday's damage. Keep recovery useful, but distinguish final harm from damage taken and reward speed or successful counters in the score. Also fix or relabel debrief statistics: “Cards played” showed 8 and “Threats blocked” showed 1 for the run, because those fields currently read only the last battle's stats.

5. **Add distinct action feedback and improve hand comparison.** Differentiate recovery, shielding, and counters with short effects and optional sound; add a brief incident-clear celebration before drafting. On mobile, only about two and a half cards fit onscreen, so comparing the hand requires scrolling. Try a compact overview with a selected-card detail view. The arena looks good, but its towers currently convey little actionable information; highlight the threatened service before adding more scenery.

## Next experiment

Start with counterplay balance and clear sudden-event feedback. Test the revised opening and a later incident with several new players. Observe whether they can explain the incoming danger, whether defensive plays become worthwhile, and whether they voluntarily start another run. Do not infer replay value from this single successful run.

## Follow-up: implemented refinement (rules version 4)

Implemented recovery penalties on Database lock, Config drift, Thundering herd, and Hot partition. Their threat panels explain how to restore contain gains, and affected cards show the penalty. Runbook now costs one focus and prepares +12 for the next contain this round; logs draws two and prepares +4. Setup stacks, is consumed once, and expires at round end. War room and All hands cost one focus; failover's strongest gain requires its matching counter. Later stability targets are 80, 85, and 90.

Surprise risk is shown beside the threat, and named impacts persist through the next round and reload. Optional synthesized sound, distinct feedback colors, and victory animation reinforce actions. The phone hand shows five compact cards with a selected-card description and explicit Play button. Score uses cumulative damage, speed, counters, and prevention; debrief totals aggregate all incidents. Local saves now copy the input list, serialize writes, and wait for transaction completion before reporting saved.
